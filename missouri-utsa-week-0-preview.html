SEASON 22 | WEEK 0 GAME PREVIEW
Columbia, Missouri

No More Excuses, No More Offseason: Missouri Opens a New Era Against UTSA

After a miserable 4–8 campaign, Coach OKST56 and the Tigers return to Faurot Field with a new offense, a new defense, and an opportunity to prove that last season belongs in the rearview mirror.

By William "Buck" McGraw
Senior Football Columnist | The Zou Prairie Press

COLUMBIA, Mo. — Well, folks, we've finally reached that wonderful time of year when football coaches have to stop explaining what they're going to do and actually start doing it.

The offseason is over.

The practices are finished.

The playbooks have been installed, the depth charts have been shuffled, and somewhere in Columbia, Coach OKST56 is preparing to find out whether all those changes were worth the trouble.

Missouri opens Season 22 on Saturday, August 29, hosting the UTSA Roadrunners at Faurot Field.

Kickoff is scheduled for 1 p.m.

It's the beginning of a new era for the SFA Dynasty, and perhaps an even more significant one for a Missouri program trying to recover from its first losing season under OKST56.

The Tigers went 8–5 in each of the coach's first two seasons before stumbling to 4–8 last year.

That leaves him with a 20–18 record entering his fourth campaign.

Twenty wins. Eighteen losses.

Respectable enough to keep a man employed.

Not quite enough to get a statue built.

Now comes UTSA, a Conference USA opponent looking to spoil Missouri's season opener.

And while the Roadrunners might not arrive in Columbia with the same expectations as some of the Big 12 heavyweights waiting later on the schedule, overlooking them would be a fine way to begin the season with a headache.

I've seen enough football to know that the most dangerous opponent is often the one you've already penciled into the win column.

The Matchup

UTSA Roadrunners (0–0) at Missouri Tigers (0–0)

Date: Saturday, August 29, 2026
Kickoff: 1:00 p.m.
Location: Faurot Field at Memorial Stadium, Columbia, Missouri
Conference: UTSA — Conference USA | Missouri — Big 12

Both teams enter the game without any Season 22 statistics.

No passing yards.

No rushing touchdowns.

No turnovers.

And, perhaps most importantly, no evidence yet that anybody's offseason promises were worth listening to.

For Missouri, this opener represents the first real test of a completely revamped football identity.

Harper Gets the Keys to the New Offense

Senior quarterback Kenny Harper enters the season positioned to lead Missouri's new Oklahoma State-inspired offense.

The Affton, Missouri, native took over the starting job midway through Season 21 after Gavin Grayson opened the year under center.

Neither quarterback managed to reverse Missouri's fortunes, with the Tigers going 2–4 during each half of the season.

But that was last year.

Harper now enters his senior campaign with a new offensive system and an opportunity to establish himself as the leader of a program searching for consistency.

Across the field, UTSA will turn to quarterback Richie Frederick.

The preseason matchup favors Harper, who brings a clear advantage in the game's player comparison.

That doesn't guarantee anything once the football is snapped, but it does place expectations squarely on the Missouri quarterback's shoulders.

Frederick will be tasked with directing the Roadrunners' offense in a hostile environment, while Harper gets the benefit of opening the season in front of his home crowd.

The Tigers' new attack figures to spread the field and create opportunities through the passing game.

I'm told that's what modern football is supposed to look like.

Personally, I still believe there's something beautiful about a running back following a pulling guard through a hole big enough to park a combine.

But if Harper starts throwing touchdowns, I'll manage to survive.

Probably.

Missouri's Receivers Have an Opportunity to Make Some Noise

One of the more intriguing aspects of Saturday's matchup is the difference between the two receiving groups.

UTSA features Melvin Owens, Joseph Rushing, and Kyle Russell among its offensive options.

Owens appears to be one of the Roadrunners' more established receiving threats, while Rushing and Russell will look to contribute to Frederick's passing attack.

Missouri counters with a group that should provide Harper with several options.

Sophomore Donte Lacey enters the season as one of the Tigers' leading receivers, while junior Taj Beamer returns to the offense.

Jeff Meyer also appears among Missouri's featured offensive personnel for the opener.

That's particularly interesting considering Meyer entered the preseason competing for playing time in the fourth or fifth receiver role.

His appearance in the matchup preview suggests he'll have an opportunity to contribute as Missouri begins installing its new offensive identity on Saturdays.

And in a spread-oriented system, those additional receiving options could become increasingly important.

The Tigers also have freshman running back Zach Baker in the backfield, providing another offensive weapon as Missouri attempts to establish balance.

The question is whether Harper can distribute the football efficiently enough to keep UTSA's defense guessing.

A spread offense can create plenty of opportunities.

It can also create a whole lot of incomplete passes if nobody remembers where the receivers are supposed to be.

UTSA Has Some Weapons of Its Own

It would be a mistake to assume Missouri's advantages at several offensive positions mean the Roadrunners have nothing to offer.

UTSA running back Jack Williams enters the matchup as one of the stronger individual players featured in the Roadrunners' offensive personnel.

Williams presents an interesting challenge for a Missouri defense transitioning to a new 4–2–5 alignment.

The Tigers will need to demonstrate that their defensive front can control the line of scrimmage while the linebackers and defensive backs adjust to their new responsibilities.

UTSA also features tight end Tyrone Johnson, another player who stands out among the Roadrunners' offensive options.

Johnson could become an important part of UTSA's attack, particularly if the Roadrunners look to challenge Missouri's linebackers and defensive backs in coverage.

That's worth watching.

Missouri spent the offseason making changes to its defensive structure, but the effectiveness of those changes won't be determined by how impressive the formation looks before the snap.

It'll be determined by whether the Tigers can actually stop somebody.

A revolutionary concept, I know.

The First Test of Missouri's New Defense

Coach OKST56's decision to adopt a 4–2–5 defense has been one of the more significant developments of the offseason.

The system will place five defensive backs on the field in its base alignment, while relying on the defensive front and linebackers to control the running game.

For Missouri, Saturday's opener offers an opportunity to begin sorting through a crowded linebacker situation.

Heggie Beckwith, Remy Lasagna, and Wayne Blanchard entered the preseason among the leading candidates for significant playing time.

With only two traditional linebacker positions in the base alignment, how the coaching staff distributes snaps will be worth monitoring.

On the edge, Daeden Hopkins is another player looking to establish himself in the defensive rotation.

The Tigers will need contributions from that group if they hope to keep UTSA from controlling the football.

The matchup also provides Missouri's secondary with its first opportunity to demonstrate how effectively it can operate in the new system.

The Tigers have personnel capable of making plays in coverage, but communication and tackling will be critical.

And if there's one thing I've learned about installing a new defense, it's that everybody looks organized until the other team starts moving.

Then things get interesting.

Sometimes in ways nobody particularly enjoys.

The Battle Up Front

Missouri enters the opener with some encouraging pieces along its offensive line.

Senior left tackle Marcus Smith is featured among the Tigers' offensive personnel and should be an important part of protecting Harper.

UTSA counters with left tackle Christian Hardin and left guard DJay Williams Jr. among its featured linemen.

The individual matchup comparison favors UTSA at left guard, while Missouri appears to have the advantage at left tackle.

For the Tigers, keeping Harper upright will be essential if the new passing attack is going to operate as intended.

It's difficult to spread the field and throw the football when your quarterback is spending most of his afternoon examining the grass.

And on the other side of the ball, Missouri's defensive front will need to prevent UTSA from establishing a comfortable rushing attack.

Games like this are often decided by the least glamorous people on the field.

The ones doing the blocking.

The ones doing the tackling.

The ones who rarely get their names in the newspaper unless something goes terribly wrong.

I've always had a soft spot for those fellows.

What Missouri Needs to Show

For Coach OKST56, this game is about considerably more than starting the season 1–0.

It's about demonstrating that the changes made during the offseason have actually addressed some of the problems that plagued last year's team.

Missouri needs Harper to look comfortable directing the new offense.

The Tigers need their receivers to create opportunities and their offensive line to provide enough protection for those opportunities to develop.

Defensively, the new 4–2–5 needs to demonstrate that it can handle UTSA's running game without leaving obvious weaknesses elsewhere.

And perhaps most importantly, Missouri needs to play a complete football game.

Last season was filled with frustration and inconsistency.

The Tigers never managed consecutive victories, and their inability to build momentum ultimately contributed to a 4–8 finish.

Saturday provides a chance to begin changing that narrative.

But there's a difference between having an opportunity and taking advantage of it.

One involves preparation.

The other involves actually doing something useful when the ball is snapped.

Buck's Final Word

On paper, Missouri has the advantage in this matchup.

The Tigers feature the stronger quarterback comparison, several promising receiving options, and the benefit of opening the season at home.

UTSA, meanwhile, brings a capable running back and some offensive personnel who could test Missouri's new defensive alignment.

The Roadrunners shouldn't be dismissed.

But this is a game Missouri should expect to win.

And after last season, that's an important distinction.

Coach OKST56 doesn't need to produce an offensive masterpiece on Saturday.

He doesn't need to convince me that throwing the football all over the field is the greatest invention since barbed wire.

And he certainly doesn't need to start making speeches about championships.

He needs his team to play sound football.

Protect the quarterback.

Tackle the ball carrier.

Avoid giving away possessions.

And, preferably, score more points than UTSA.

I realize that last one sounds painfully obvious.

But after watching enough college football, I've learned that obvious things occasionally need to be explained.

Missouri's fourth season under OKST56 begins Saturday afternoon.

The Tigers have a new offense, a new defense, and a fresh opportunity to prove that last year's struggles were an unfortunate exception.

The excuses have run out.

The scoreboard is about to turn on.

And I've got a feeling we're going to learn quite a bit about this football team.

Here's hoping the first lesson is a pleasant one.

William "Buck" McGraw
