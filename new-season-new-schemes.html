SEASON 22 | INAUGURAL PRESEASON EDITION
Columbia, Missouri

New Season, New Schemes, Same Old Questions in Columbia

After consecutive eight-win seasons gave way to a 4–8 disaster, Coach OKST56 is betting on a new offensive identity, a rebuilt defensive approach, and a senior quarterback to bring Missouri back from the brink.

By William "Buck" McGraw
Senior Football Columnist | The Zou Prairie Press

COLUMBIA, Mo. — There's an old saying around these parts: If your tractor ain't running right, sometimes you've got to get under the hood.

Apparently, Coach OKST56 has decided to replace the whole damn engine.

Missouri enters Season 22 of the SFA Dynasty with a new offensive playbook, a new defensive scheme, and a football program searching for answers after a 4–8 campaign that made the previous two seasons feel like distant memories.

The Tigers went 8–5 in both Season 19 and Season 20, OKST56's first two years in Columbia. It wasn't championship football, but it was winning football. And in this business, winning eight games is a considerably better way to spend your autumn than losing eight.

Then came Season 21.

Four wins. Eight losses. And a whole lot of Saturdays that would've been better spent fixing fence posts.

Now, with a 20–18 career record at Missouri, Coach OKST56 enters his fourth season with the pressure mounting and the playbook changing.

The SFA itself is entering a new generation of college football after 21 seasons on familiar ground. New surroundings, new possibilities, and, presumably, entirely new ways for a football team to ruin a perfectly good Saturday.

A Quarterback Controversy With a Familiar Ending

Perhaps no position will draw more attention this preseason than quarterback, where senior Kenny Harper and sophomore Gavin Grayson return after splitting last year's starting duties.

Grayson, an 80-overall sophomore from Vancouver, Washington, opened Season 21 under center. Through six games, Missouri managed a 2–4 record, including victories over Charlotte and Colorado.

Then came the change.

Harper took over for the final six contests, guiding the Tigers to victories over Iowa State and Purdue.

His record?

Also 2–4.

So, for those keeping score at home, Missouri changed quarterbacks halfway through the season and finished both halves with precisely the same record.

That's the kind of improvement that makes a man check whether his calendar is upside down.

But entering Season 22, the picture looks different.

Harper is now a 93-overall senior from Affton, Missouri, and the highest-rated quarterback on the roster. He's positioned to lead an offense that will look substantially different from the one Missouri fielded a season ago.

The Tigers are adopting the Oklahoma State offensive playbook, bringing a more spread-oriented attack to Columbia.

For a team looking to rediscover its offensive identity, the change is intriguing.

For an old cattleman who believes a good running game is one of life's great pleasures, it's mildly concerning.

I'm told this modern approach involves spreading everybody across the field and throwing the football until the defense gets tired of chasing it.

Personally, I've always believed the easiest way to move a football ten yards is to hand it to somebody who can run eleven.

But perhaps I'm getting old.

Plenty of Targets, Plenty to Prove

The receiving corps could become one of the more interesting position groups in Missouri's new offense.

Sophomore Donte Lacey, an 80-overall receiver from Cheviot, Ohio, leads the position group in overall rating. Freshman Chris Cole, a 79-overall prospect from Kankakee, Illinois, follows closely behind.

Returning junior Taj Beamer, a 78-overall receiver from Calgary, provides another option as Missouri prepares to spread the football around.

Behind them, redshirt sophomore Brent Amos (77 overall) joins Jeff Meyer (75) and freshman John Rhodes (73) in a group where playing time remains up for grabs.

Meyer, a junior from Lufkin, Texas, and Rhodes, a freshman from Rapid Valley, South Dakota, are expected to battle for opportunities in the fourth and fifth receiver spots.

With Missouri's new offensive philosophy, those positions could matter considerably more than their depth-chart labels suggest.

Meanwhile, the backfield features freshman Zach Baker, a 78-overall running back from Tustin, California, and redshirt junior Tim Weber, a 75-overall back from Oakville, Missouri.

Whether the Tigers can find a balance between the ground game and their new passing attack will be worth watching.

After all, there's nothing wrong with throwing the football.

I'd just prefer somebody remember that running it is still legal.

Five Defensive Backs and a Whole Lot of Questions

While the offensive overhaul will command much of the attention, Missouri's move to a 4–2–5 defense could prove equally significant.

The new alignment features four defensive linemen, two linebackers, and five defensive backs, creating some intriguing decisions for the coaching staff.

At linebacker, competition is expected to be fierce.

Sophomore Heggie Beckwith, a 72-overall defender from Nashua, New Hampshire, and junior Remy Lasagna, also rated 72 overall, from Jefferson City, Missouri, enter the preseason among the leading candidates for significant playing time.

Redshirt junior Wayne Blanchard, a 71-overall linebacker from Joplin, Missouri, is firmly in the conversation as well.

Three linebackers. Closely matched ratings. Two traditional linebacker spots in the base defense.

Somebody is going to have to earn his supper.

And with other linebackers competing for roles, the coaching staff will have no shortage of decisions to make before the season begins.

On the defensive edge, redshirt freshman Daeden Hopkins is another name worth remembering.

The Hermann, Missouri, native enters the season at 73 overall, matching the ratings of several other defensive ends competing for opportunities.

Hopkins will be fighting for a starting role, and the competition could be one of the more consequential battles of the preseason.

Behind the defensive front, sophomore cornerback Bill Bangs stands out as one of Missouri's highest-rated defensive players at 85 overall. The Kill Devil Hills, North Carolina, native figures to be an important piece of the Tigers' new defensive structure.

Junior free safety Drew Chavis, a 78-overall defender from O'Fallon, Missouri, and redshirt senior Duane Washington, rated 77 overall, provide additional options in the secondary.

The talent is there to make things interesting.

But as I've said for years, a new defensive formation doesn't automatically make anybody tackle better.

You can draw eleven men anywhere you please on a chalkboard.

Eventually, somebody still has to put the ball carrier on the ground.

The Big Boys and the Man Who Kicks It Away

Missouri's offensive line features two notable senior tackles.

Right tackle Nate Austin, an 85-overall lineman from Brownsville, Texas, is the highest-rated offensive lineman on the roster. On the opposite side, senior Marcus Smith, rated 82 overall and hailing from Carrollton, Texas, gives Missouri another experienced option.

Their work could go a long way toward determining whether the new passing attack succeeds.

Because no matter how fancy the offensive playbook gets, a quarterback still needs enough time to throw the football.

And then there's junior punter Jake Graham.

The Levelland, Texas, native enters Season 22 with a 94 overall rating, making him the highest-rated player on Missouri's roster.

That's right, folks.

The highest-rated Tiger is the man whose job is to give the football to the other team.

Now, I'm a firm believer in good special teams. Always have been.

But here's hoping Missouri's best player doesn't end up being its busiest.

The Fourth Year Is Where Excuses Get Expensive

There's no avoiding the importance of Season 22.

Coach OKST56 inherited the Missouri job ahead of Season 19 and promptly delivered back-to-back 8–5 campaigns.

Those seasons established a reasonable expectation that Missouri could compete and win games in the Big 12.

Then came last year's collapse.

The Tigers opened Season 21 with three consecutive losses before defeating Charlotte. They eventually picked up wins over Colorado, Iowa State, and Purdue, but never managed to string together consecutive victories.

They finished the year with losses to Oklahoma, Kansas, and Kansas State.

A 4–8 finish is disappointing enough.

A 4–8 finish after consecutive eight-win seasons raises a more uncomfortable question: Was last year an unfortunate detour, or did the program take a wrong turn somewhere outside Columbia?

The new schemes provide a fresh start.

The senior quarterback provides experience.

And the roster offers several intriguing position battles that could shape the season.

But the Big 12 isn't going to hand Missouri victories because somebody installed a different playbook.

The Tigers have to earn them.

A New Voice in Tiger Country

As Missouri prepares for its 22nd season in the SFA, The Zou Prairie Press begins its own journey covering the Tigers.

We'll report the victories, examine the defeats, and ask the questions that need asking.

We'll praise good football when we see it.

We'll call bad football what it is.

And we'll do our best not to pretend that completing a four-yard pass on third-and-nine represents meaningful offensive progress.

As for Coach OKST56's new offensive philosophy?

I'll admit I'm curious.

Maybe this wide-open passing business really is the future of football. Maybe Harper will spend his senior season tossing touchdowns around Faurot Field like a farmer scattering feed.

Maybe Missouri will score so many points that I'll have to retire my complaints about the forward pass.

Or maybe we'll spend the next three months watching footballs sail over receivers' heads while somebody explains how the underlying numbers are actually encouraging.

Either way, we'll be watching.

And if the Tigers start winning games by throwing the ball all over creation, I'll be the first to admit I was wrong.

Well, perhaps the second.

Let's not get carried away.

William "Buck" McGraw
