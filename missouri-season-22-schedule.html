Twelve Games, Two Bye Weeks, and Not Much Room for Error: Missouri's Season 22 Schedule Is Here

The Tigers open against UTSA, face a brutal stretch of road games, and close the regular season with three consecutive home contests. For Coach OKST56, the road back from 4–8 won't be particularly forgiving.

By William "Buck" McGraw
Senior Football Columnist | The Zou Prairie Press

COLUMBIA, Mo. — There's something special about the release of a college football schedule.

Every team is undefeated. Every coach is optimistic. Every fan has already identified six guaranteed victories and three games the officials are probably going to steal.

It's a wonderful time of year.

Unfortunately, they eventually make everybody play the games.

Missouri's Season 22 schedule is officially set, and Coach OKST56's fourth campaign in Columbia will feature 12 regular-season contests, two bye weeks, and a collection of opponents that should provide a fairly thorough examination of whether the Tigers have actually improved.

After finishing 4–8 last season, Missouri enters the year looking to recover from its worst campaign under OKST56.

The good news? There are opportunities on this schedule.

The bad news? Several of them involve getting on a bus or airplane and playing football in places where the home crowd would prefer Missouri leave disappointed.

And judging by the middle of this schedule, the Tigers might want to pack an extra lunch.

An Early Start and a Chance to Set the Tone

Missouri won't have to wait until September to begin its season.

The Tigers open Week 0 on Saturday, August 29, hosting UTSA at Faurot Field.

For a program installing Oklahoma State's offensive playbook and transitioning to a 4–2–5 defense, the early opener represents an important opportunity to establish some rhythm.

It also means Missouri gets to begin making mistakes a week earlier than much of the country.

I'm sure the coaching staff is thrilled.

Arizona visits Columbia the following Saturday before the Tigers head west for a September 12 meeting with Colorado.

That Colorado matchup carries some recent significance.

Missouri defeated the Buffaloes during last season's difficult 4–8 campaign, making it one of just four victories the Tigers managed all year.

Repeating that result would give OKST56 a useful early-season measuring stick.

The Tigers then travel to Eastern Michigan on September 19.

On paper, the opening four games offer Missouri a chance to build momentum before the schedule becomes considerably less hospitable.

And make no mistake, building momentum matters.

A team coming off eight losses doesn't need to spend September discovering new and creative ways to fall behind by two touchdowns.

September Ends With a Trip to Iowa

Missouri's first particularly imposing test arrives September 26 with a trip to No. 18 Iowa.

The Hawkeyes are one of three preseason-ranked opponents appearing on the Tigers' schedule.

Playing a ranked opponent on the road is difficult enough.

Doing it in the middle of a stretch featuring three consecutive away games is another matter entirely.

Colorado. Eastern Michigan. Iowa.

Three Saturdays. Three road trips.

That's a lot of travel for a football team still adjusting to new offensive and defensive systems.

I've driven across enough of the Midwest to know that spending three straight weekends away from home is a fine way to develop an appreciation for your own mattress.

Whether Missouri develops an appreciation for winning on the road remains to be seen.

Oklahoma State Comes to Columbia

October begins with an intriguing matchup.

On October 3, Missouri welcomes Oklahoma State to Faurot Field.

And yes, that's the same Oklahoma State whose offensive playbook Coach OKST56 has adopted for Season 22.

There's something wonderfully awkward about inviting somebody over to your house and showing them you've been borrowing their ideas.

Of course, running the same playbook doesn't guarantee the same results.

Personnel, execution, and decision-making still determine whether an offense succeeds.

But the matchup should provide an interesting storyline as Missouri's new offensive identity takes shape.

It also arrives at a critical point in the season.

With a trip to Norman looming the following week, the Tigers would benefit greatly from taking care of business at home.

Because what comes next isn't exactly a relaxing autumn getaway.

Oklahoma, Iowa State, and Texas A&M: The Stretch That Could Define the Season

October 10.

Norman, Oklahoma.

No. 10 Oklahoma.

That's where Missouri's schedule takes a particularly unpleasant turn.

The Sooners enter the season ranked among the nation's top teams, and the Tigers will have to face them on the road.

Missouri also closed last season with a loss to Oklahoma, making this another opportunity to measure how far the program has progressed.

Or, depending on the outcome, how much further it still needs to go.

Fortunately, the Tigers receive their first bye week immediately afterward.

Unfortunately, whoever arranged the schedule apparently decided one difficult road game wasn't enough.

Missouri returns to action October 24 at Iowa State, followed by a Friday night trip to No. 8 Texas A&M on October 30.

That's three consecutive road games surrounding the first bye week, with two top-ten opponents appearing within a three-game stretch.

Oklahoma.

Iowa State.

Texas A&M.

Two of those teams are ranked in the preseason top ten, and all three will host the Tigers.

If Missouri emerges from that stretch in good shape, it'll have earned every bit of it.

If not, well, I've got a feeling the letters section of this newspaper will be getting considerably more interesting.

The Texas A&M matchup is particularly notable because it takes place on a Friday night.

Nothing says a pleasant end to October quite like spending your Friday evening in College Station trying to knock off the nation's eighth-ranked team.

I'd recommend bringing a jacket.

And perhaps a backup plan.

Three Home Games to Finish the Job

After a second bye week on November 7, Missouri returns home for the final three games of its regular season.

Baylor visits November 14.

Kansas State follows November 21.

And then comes Kansas.

The Tigers and Jayhawks will meet November 28 at Faurot Field in a rivalry matchup that should require very little additional motivation.

Three consecutive home games to close the season is a favorable arrangement, particularly after Missouri's difficult travel schedule earlier in the year.

But the opponents shouldn't be overlooked.

Missouri lost to Kansas State and Kansas during its final stretch last season.

Now both programs return to Columbia, offering the Tigers opportunities to change the narrative.

The Kansas game, in particular, has all the ingredients of a memorable regular-season finale.

A rivalry opponent.

A home crowd.

And potentially significant implications for how Missouri's fourth season under OKST56 will be remembered.

There's a certain satisfaction in ending the year by beating your rival.

There's also a particular kind of misery in spending the offseason remembering that you didn't.

I've experienced enough of the latter on behalf of football fans to know which one I'd recommend.

Six Home Games, Six Road Games, and Two Very Different Halves

Perhaps the most interesting feature of Missouri's schedule is how unevenly its home and road games are distributed.

The Tigers play six games at Faurot Field and six away from Columbia.

That's balanced enough.

But look closer.

After hosting UTSA and Arizona to begin the season, Missouri plays six of its next seven games on the road, with Oklahoma State providing the lone home contest during that stretch.

Six of seven.

That's not a road trip.

That's a traveling circus with shoulder pads.

Then, after the second bye week, the Tigers finish with three straight home games.

It's a schedule that places enormous importance on surviving the middle of the season.

Missouri doesn't necessarily need to win every difficult road matchup to put together a successful campaign.

But the Tigers cannot afford to let one disappointing Saturday turn into three or four.

That's how seasons unravel.

And after last year's 4–8 finish, this program should have a pretty good understanding of what unraveling looks like.

What Does Success Look Like?

Coach OKST56 enters Season 22 with a 20–18 record at Missouri.

His first two seasons produced identical 8–5 finishes.

His third produced four victories.

The objective now is straightforward: prove that Season 21 was a setback rather than a warning.

The schedule offers opportunities for improvement, particularly early and late in the year.

But the middle is demanding, featuring three ranked opponents, extensive travel, and a stretch of games that could test Missouri's depth and composure.

The Tigers will need their new offense to develop quickly.

They'll need the 4–2–5 defense to hold up against Big 12 competition.

And they'll need to avoid the kind of inconsistency that plagued last season.

There's a path to a winning record here.

There's also a path to another long autumn.

The difference will have considerably more to do with blocking, tackling, and execution than anything printed on a preseason schedule.

Buck's Final Word

I've studied Missouri's schedule, poured myself another cup of coffee, and come to one fairly simple conclusion.

The Tigers have been given every opportunity to prove what kind of football team they intend to be.

They'll have a chance to establish themselves early.

They'll face serious adversity in the middle.

And they'll return home with three opportunities to finish the job.

That's about as fair an examination as a football program can ask for.

Of course, fair doesn't necessarily mean pleasant.

Coach OKST56 has spent the offseason changing his offense, reorganizing his defense, and preparing for a new era of SFA football.

Now the schedule is out.

The opponents are waiting.

And somewhere in Columbia, somebody is probably already explaining how the Tigers could realistically win ten games.

Maybe they're right.

But after last season, I'd suggest starting with five.

The season opens August 29 against UTSA.

Until then, everybody's undefeated.

Enjoy it while it lasts.
