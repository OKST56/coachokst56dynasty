A New Paper, an Old Cowboy, and a Football Program Worth Writing About

After 21 seasons of SFA football, The Zou Prairie Press opens its doors in Columbia. Its newest columnist has a few things to say, and not all of them will be particularly pleasant.

By William "Buck" McGraw

Senior Football Columnist | The Zou Prairie Press

COLUMBIA, Mo. — I've spent the better part of my life learning three things about Missouri.

The weather can't be trusted. Cattle are generally smarter than people give them credit for. And college football has an extraordinary ability to turn perfectly reasonable grown men into emotional wrecks.

That last one, as it turns out, is good for business.

My name is William McGraw, though most folks have called me Buck since I was old enough to know better. I grew up outside Sedalia, Missouri, where I learned to throw a football, mend a fence, and recognize the difference between a legitimate excuse and a steaming pile of manure.

It's a distinction that's served me particularly well in sports journalism.

I've spent more than 25 years covering football, and somewhere along the way I discovered that the sport has a remarkable talent for producing both extraordinary moments and spectacular incompetence.

Sometimes on consecutive plays.

Now, as the SFA Dynasty prepares to begin its 22nd season, I'm pleased to introduce The Zou Prairie Press, an independent publication dedicated to covering Missouri Tigers football.

And before anybody asks, no, we're not here to be the university's public relations department.

They've already got people for that.

Twenty-One Seasons of Football, and Plenty Left to Say

The SFA has been around long enough to develop its own history, rivalries, traditions, and collection of coaches who probably wish certain games had never been recorded.

Twenty-one seasons.

That's a considerable amount of football.

Enough to build dynasties, establish reputations, and discover that some coaches are remarkably committed to making the same mistakes year after year.

Missouri has been part of that story, competing in the Big 12 and navigating the same unpredictable world of college football as everybody else.

The Tigers have had their share of seasons worth remembering, along with a few that probably deserve to be sealed in a box and buried somewhere outside Boone County.

But history matters.

Not because it guarantees anything about tomorrow, but because it provides a useful measuring stick for the people making promises today.

And around here, we're going to keep track.

The OKST56 Era: Progress, Promise, and a Pothole

For Missouri, the current chapter began in Season 19, when Coach OKST56 took over the program.

His first season ended with an 8–5 record.

The following year?

Another 8–5.

Two consecutive winning seasons. Sixteen victories. A respectable foundation for a program looking to establish itself in the Big 12.

Nothing wrong with that.

I've always maintained that consistency is an admirable quality, provided you're consistently doing something worth admiring.

Then came Season 21.

Four wins. Eight losses.

And suddenly that foundation looked like something a contractor might want to inspect before putting another story on top of it.

Missouri failed to win consecutive games throughout the season, changed starting quarterbacks halfway through the year, and ultimately finished with twice as many losses as victories.

Not exactly the kind of campaign that gets commemorated on a stadium wall.

Through three seasons, Coach OKST56 owns a 20–18 record in Columbia.

That's a winning percentage of .526, for those inclined to put numbers on things.

For those who aren't, it means the Tigers have won slightly more often than they've lost.

Not a disaster.

Not a dynasty.

And certainly not enough to start engraving trophies.

Season 22 will mark the coach's fourth year at Missouri, and there's reason to believe this could be an important one.

A new offensive philosophy is coming to Columbia. The defense is getting an overhaul. And the entire SFA is stepping into a new era after 21 seasons of familiar football.

Whether any of that translates into more victories remains to be seen.

I've learned over the years that a new playbook is a lot like a new pickup truck.

Everybody's impressed until it gets stuck in the same mud hole as the old one.

What You Can Expect From This Newspaper

Let me make something clear from the beginning.

I like Missouri football.

I want the Tigers to win.

I want Faurot Field to be a miserable place for visiting teams, the Big 12 to dread trips to Columbia, and opposing coaches to spend their Sunday mornings wondering what in God's name happened the night before.

But liking a football program and blindly defending it are two very different things.

When Missouri plays good football, we'll say so.

When somebody makes a remarkable play, we'll give him the recognition he's earned.

When Coach OKST56 makes a decision that wins a football game, we'll give him credit.

And when the Tigers do something so profoundly stupid that it leaves half the fan base staring silently at their television screens, we'll be discussing that too.

In considerable detail.

The Zou Prairie Press will cover the games, the players, the coaching decisions, the rivalries, and the stories that make this league worth following.

We'll examine the numbers, revisit the history, and occasionally ask questions that coaches would rather answer with some variation of "we've just got to execute better."

That's fine.

We'll ask again.

We're not here to manufacture controversy or invent stories.

Football provides plenty of material on its own.

And we're certainly not here to pretend every loss is a moral victory.

I've never seen a moral victory listed in the Big 12 standings.

A Word About the Man Behind the Typewriter

Some of you may find my opinions old-fashioned.

That's fair.

I believe a good offensive line is worth its weight in gold. I believe a running back ought to be able to get a yard when everybody in the stadium knows he's getting the football. And I believe a defense that can't tackle has considerably larger problems than its formation.

I'm also told the modern game is moving toward spreading the field and throwing the football all over creation.

Maybe so.

I've got nothing against progress.

I just prefer that it show up on the scoreboard before somebody asks me to celebrate it.

And if Missouri proves that this newfangled passing business can win football games, I'll gladly reconsider my position.

I may even write something nice about it.

Stranger things have happened.

Not many, but a few.

Here's to Season 22

So here we are.

A new season. A new era of SFA football. A Missouri program looking to recover from a difficult year.

And a new newspaper prepared to document every glorious, miserable, bewildering moment of it.

I can't promise the Tigers will win the Big 12.

I can't promise Coach OKST56 will get everything figured out.

And I certainly can't promise that watching Missouri football won't occasionally make you question your decision to become emotionally invested in a bunch of young men chasing an oddly shaped ball.

But I can promise this.

We'll tell the story as it happens.

We'll remember what came before.

We'll give credit where it's due, criticism where it's earned, and absolutely no sympathy to a coach who punts from his opponent's 38-yard line while trailing in the fourth quarter.

I've got a fresh pot of coffee, a sharpened pencil, and 21 seasons of history to catch up on.

The cattle have been fed.

The press is rolling.

And somewhere in Columbia, somebody is already preparing to make a decision I'll have to write about.

Welcome to The Zou Prairie Press.

I suspect we're going to get along just fine.

Unless you're the offensive coordinator.

William "Buck" McGraw

Senior Football Columnist
